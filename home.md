How can we design working systems without a fabrication shop? **By integrating powerful off-the-shelf components** together with 3D printed designs. Openbox is a whole ecosystem to demonstrate the power of engineered OTS parts in a designer's hands.  Explore the inventions based on a "handy box," a cheap standard part found in American hardware stores.  This box is a powerful tool for it's thermal properties, rigidity, dead weight, and availability of attachments that assemble effortlessly.  OpenBox is here to crack open your imagination as a designer.

## Mechanical
How do engineers design moving, mechanical machines? Mechanical parts must support motion and load-bearing simultaneously and continuously.  That demands rigidity, toughness, and geometry that suits alignment of components like shafts and gears.  OpenBox meets all of these needs for a mechanical designer.  Below, find the box utilized as a housing for large bearings and a heavy rotating chuck.  This design was built to see if the box can hold a perpendicular shaft and keep it's shape under loads, like this spinning mass.

- ![photo, lathe chuck OPEN](img/use_chuck1.jpg)
- ![photo, lathe chuck CLOSED](img/use_chuck2.jpg)
- ![photo, lathe chuck inside](img/use_chuck3.jpg)

How can we build a chain drive mechanism with just a 3D printer?  The box is a huge help where the pre-made knockouts align shafts and carry forces from the coupling chain.  This demonstration piece has a printed knob at the end of the M8 bolt, so we can spin the assembly by hand.

## Interfacing
The OpenBox project is designed to document and standardize specifications for small utility boxes commonly used in various countries. While the 4” x 2” x 1-7/8” (16.5 cubic inches) dimensions serve as a relative standard for the small utility box, specifications can vary depending on manufacturers and regions.

For a detailed overview of brand-specific designs and variations, refer to the [Design Catalog](design.md). This resource compiles technical sheets, dimensional drawings, and reliable documentation from various manufacturere..

The message here is for engineering designers.  (Other readers are welcome but the expectations for engineers is higher) 
 
Initiating OpenBox is part of my action towards a life goal of improving the resources for design engineers, and enhancing our outputs so we can deliver more value in the world.
For this goal, I'm communicating prompts and suggestions on my youtube channel.  These are some of the prompts:
If an engineer asks - what is this website for? What is the purpose of openbox, and trying to share and distribute these design elements?
1) STOP CREATING PROJECTS FROM NOTHING:  Build your technology on top of existing works. Discover where engineers created value and learn to leverage it. Projects where good designs are made already.
2) STOP ISOLATING 3D printing as your only tool! If we make fully-printed assemblies, we are trashing good solutions by failing to use metal. We throw out 100 years of development by NOT including parts like OpenBox.
3) STOP printing enclosures!  3D printing designs are inferior for large, flat, boxlike products.  Find an appropriate OTS enclosure!
4) STOP making designs alone.  START your assembly design from one that is published.
5) STOP publishing designs that lack the designer data. STOP posting on thingiverse and take control of your own data USING github.
6) STOP using a 3D printer to create things that have ONE variant.  Low mix high volume is for INJECTION MOULDING.  3D printing is for CUSTOMIZATION and it should be leveraged or we lose the purpose of printing.

For each prompt above, someone may ask:
1) How?  Use OpenBox as an example of how to build on other designs. You can build from the OTS parts, or even build from the assemblies shared here!
2) How? Copy from the methods in OpenBox. Inside each design you'll find answers on how the OTS part was integrated, mated, and designed into the feature.
3) How? Obviously openbox shows enclosures created just by printing a top cover.  It's an example of constraining your work to one base part while maintaining broad freedom for types of outcomes.
4) How? One way is to source components with details published online, just like the RACO engineers published data for the handybox.  A second way is to download the OpenBox parts and take them further.  These two paths are two huge steps ahead.
5) How? Openbox is your example to show what should be included for designers to succeed in collaborating.  This website (github repo) includes ALL the data to redesign what is shared.  The base geometry, the expressed constraints, the measurements and photos of the desired results.
6) How? OpenBox shows several ways to do this.  3D printing is reserved for the _portion of the design which varies_ as the functional needs vary.  Observe and discover where the base part designs stop and the custom designs begin.  Observe how filenames are formed. How version numbers are implemented. How improvements can be delivered to two assemblies by update of one component.
